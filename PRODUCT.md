# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Quill is for privacy-conscious writers, developers, researchers, and knowledge workers who spend much of their day typing and want voice input without sending speech to a cloud service. The primary workflow happens inside another application: the user holds or locks one of two global hotkeys, speaks, and expects text to appear at the active cursor.

## Product Purpose

Quill makes local voice dictation feel immediate without forcing one cleanup behavior on every thought. Dictation mode preserves every spoken word in near real time; Scribe mode briefly waits, resolves verbal self-corrections, removes filler, and commits only the final intended wording. Success means users can move between those behaviors by muscle memory, without opening Quill.

## Positioning

Two always-available hotkeys make raw transcription and self-correcting transcription equally immediate, entirely on the user's machine.

## Brand Personality

Lucid, kinetic, trustworthy. Quill should feel like a finely tuned input instrument: responsive enough to disappear during use, explicit about what it is doing, and quietly opinionated about privacy.

## Anti-references

Quill is an independent product and must not reproduce the name, branding, interface, illustration language, or marketing structure of Wispr Flow, Willow Voice, WhimprFlow, or Svara. It should also avoid generic AI-product gradients, glowing orbs, glass panels, editorial-serif affectation, faux terminal decoration, bento feature grids, and over-rounded card stacks.

## Design Principles

- The hotkey is the mode switch; the interface must reinforce muscle memory instead of adding workflow.
- Show the difference between raw and resolved speech with real transcript behavior, not abstract AI claims.
- Keep local processing legible: users should always know the active mode, model, backend, and privacy boundary.
- Familiar controls earn trust in the product; distinctive typography, rhythm, and motion carry the brand.
- Progressive disclosure keeps first-run setup calm while advanced model and dictionary controls remain available.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for both the settings UI and marketing site. All functions must be keyboard accessible, mode must never be communicated by color alone, text must remain usable at 200% zoom, and motion must respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Hotkey choices must account for motor accessibility and conflicts with assistive technology.
